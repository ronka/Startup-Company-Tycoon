Leadership cards — UI prototype

Three redesign options for the C-level seat cards on the Team tab (src/components/game/clevel-card.tsx). Nothing is implemented yet — pick one and I'll build it.

What's wrong with the card today

  • The hierarchy is inverted. On a seat that is already filled, the loudest thing on the card is a full-width blue button that only browses the market. The person you hired is 14px grey text above it.
  • The Fire button is a broken-looking stub. styles.filled sets alignItems:'flex-start', so both buttons shrink-wrap their label — "Look at the market" comes out wide and "Fire" comes out narrow. It reads as a layout bug, not a choice.
  • Three seats × two 56px-tall buttons = ~340px of button at the bottom of an already-long screen.
  • All the character is thrown away on hire. hired already carries tier, exEmployer and personality — the picker shows them, the card renders none of them. An elite ex-Foogle CFO looks identical to a bootcamp grad.
  • Empty and filled seats weigh the same. An empty seat is the only one where acting is the point, and it gets the same treatment as a settled one.

Where the cards sit on the screen

The leadership grid is the last of five blocks. Whatever we choose has to sit under the payroll card without out-shouting it — that context is the dim part of the frame below.

Variant A — Roster row cheapest

Keep the full-size buttons, fix the hierarchy and the widths

Person first: a tier-tinted monogram, the name at full size, the tier badge, the ex-employer. The perk becomes a green chip so the thing you're paying for reads as a benefit rather than as more grey text. Both buttons go full-width in a row — primary flexes, Fire is fixed — so the stub disappears. Empty seats keep the loud blue CTA; filled seats demote it to secondary, because on a filled seat browsing is the quieter action.

Phone — every card state

Implementation: clevel-card.tsx only. No changes to primary-button.tsx, no changes to searchLabel(), no new state fields.

Variant B — Portrait card recommended

The exec is the card; the actions are a quiet footer

A filled seat becomes a small dossier — monogram, name, tier, ex-employer, the personality line that currently dies inside the picker, then perk and salary as a chip row. Actions drop to a compact footer: "Market" as a small secondary, "Fire" as a small danger-ghost. That reclaims about 90px per filled seat and makes a legend hire actually look like a trophy — gold badge and gold monogram, echoing the picker's ★ legend banner. Empty seats invert it: dashed placeholder plus one full-size blue CTA, so the seats that still need you are the loudest thing in the section.

Phone — every card state

Implementation: clevel-card.tsx, plus a size="sm" variant on primary-button.tsx (shared component — small additive change, no call sites break), plus shortening searchLabel() in team.tsx: "Look at the market" → "Market", "View candidates" → "Candidates", "Spin" stays. danger button variant needed for Fire.

Variant C — One leadership card, three rows biggest change

Collapse three cards into one roster

The three seats stop being three cards and become three rows of one card, the way a real org chart reads. Tapping a row opens that seat's picker — the whole row is the button, so there's no CTA at all on filled seats. Fire moves into the picker sheet (it belongs next to "hire", not on the roster). This is the most compact by a wide margin and the section stops competing with payroll. The cost is that Fire gets buried a tap deeper and we lose the wide 2–3-across layout entirely.

One decision baked into all three

Only elite and legend get colour. There are six tiers (desperate, personal, scrappy, mid, elite, legend). Giving each its own colour turns the Team tab into a rainbow and makes a bootcamp grad look as decorated as a legend. So: legend = gold (matching the picker's ★ banner), elite = accent blue, and everything below is a plain grey badge. Rarity stays rare.

If you'd rather every tier be individually colour-coded, say so — it's a one-line map change, but I'd argue against it.

My recommendation

Variant B. It fixes all five problems, it's the only one that surfaces tier / exEmployer / personality — data you already generate and currently throw away the moment someone is hired — and it makes the legend hire feel earned. The extra cost over A is one additive size prop and three shorter button labels.

Variant A if you want the smallest possible diff. Variant C if the screen length is the thing bothering you most and you're happy for Fire to live inside the picker.
